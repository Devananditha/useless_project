## 1. Demonstration Evaluation Framework

In a competitive hackathon setting with a **strict 5-minute presentation window** and judging panels comprising both deep AI researchers and commercial executive judges, demonstration efficacy is often the deciding factor.

This audit establishes a rigorous, side-by-side comparison of **CrossBorder TitleGuard (Concept C1)** and **PartsRescue AI (Theme 1 UC04/01)** under identical hackathon constraints:
* **Equal Development Horizon:** Student team of 2–4 members over a hackathon sprint.
* **Equal Interface Medium:** Modern web-based frontend (Next.js / React / Tailwind CSS).
* **Equal Backend Reality:** Realistic synthetic fixtures, mock APIs, and real LLM/VLM API orchestration.
* **Equal Scenario Count:** Exactly three structured, progressive live demonstration scenarios.

## 4. Key Strengths & Failure Modes Analysis

### 4.1 CrossBorder TitleGuard
* **Demo Superpower:** **Visceral Comprehension in 10 Seconds.**
  * The moment a judge sees a scanned, skewed paper title on the left screen and a bright red bounding box highlighting the missing notary seal on the right with the statutory citation (`A.R.S. § 28-2058`), they instantly understand what the software does. There is zero cognitive fatigue.
* **Demo Vulnerability:** **Appearing as "Just an OCR Wrapper."**
  * If the presenting team fails to explain the deterministic AST statutory rules engine, an AI researcher judge might dismiss it as a simple prompt sent to Claude 3.5 Sonnet. The pitch must emphasize the neuro-symbolic separation: VLM extracts structured tokens; deterministic AST executes the legal code.

### 4.2 PartsRescue AI
* **Demo Superpower:** **Commanding Multi-Agent Sophistication.**
  * Demonstrating an autonomous agent dynamically evaluating a trade-off curve between a $15 hotshot courier fee versus an $85 idle bay cost, then sending an interactive approval card to a customer's phone, demonstrates true cutting-edge agentic workflow engineering.
* **Demo Vulnerability:** **Cognitive Overload & "Table Fatigue."**
  * Showing parts tables, supersession part numbers (e.g. `XR3Z-8501-AA` $\to$ `XR3Z-8501-AB`), and simulated dispatch boards can feel cluttered and unengaging on a presentation screen. Non-automotive judges may struggle to follow the intricacies of technician flag hours and service dispatch queues within a 5-minute presentation.

## 5. Audit Conclusion

Both concepts possess exceptional demonstration potential. TitleGuard holds a modest edge (**4.65 vs 4.43**) in overall presentation punch, visual clarity, and stage reliability, while PartsRescue AI offers unmatched depth in agentic reasoning and interactive tool orchestration.
